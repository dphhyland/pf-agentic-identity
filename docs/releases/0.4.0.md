# 0.4.0 - Phase 1: blockers closed or mitigated, guard rails up

0.4.0 is Phase 1 of the production programme, in progress. Each package adds its own section here as it
lands; the "Before you deploy" list is the union of what they ask of a consumer.

**Read "Before you deploy" first.** Several changes stop things working until something is done.

## Before you deploy

1. **A production Redis URL must be `rediss://`** (S3a, PR #TBD). `MiniRedisClient` refuses `redis://` unless
   `OIDF_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE=development`, because the password in `AUTH` would otherwise cross the network in
   the clear. If you do nothing and your `OIDF_REDIS_URL` (or `REDIS_URL`) is plaintext, every store accessor
   throws from the first request: the token endpoint answers 500 `server_error` to attested clients, the
   challenge endpoint and the attester answer 500 (a configuration error, not an outage, so not the 503 of
   item 3), and `server.log` carries one ERROR naming the variable. Move to `rediss://`; the server's certificate must chain to the JVM's CAs or to the PEM file
   `OIDF_REDIS_CA_FILE` names, and must name the URL's host (an IP literal is checked against the address).
2. **Redis keys move to new namespaces** (S3a). 0.3.0 wrote `oidf:challenge:*` and `oidf:jti:*`; 0.4.0 writes
   `oidf:as:*`, `oidf:cas:*` and `oidf:fed:endpoint:*` (and reserves `oidf:admin:dpop:*`) and reads nothing
   under the old prefixes. The old keys are orphaned until their own TTL expires them: 300 s for a challenge,
   360 s for a proof `jti`, 600 s for a federation client assertion. During a rolling upgrade a `jti` spent on a
   0.3.0 node is unknown to a 0.4.0 node for that long, so a proof could be presented once to each version;
   drain the 0.3.0 nodes before starting the first 0.4.0 node if that matters to you. Nothing needs flushing,
   and `FLUSHDB` would also drop unconsumed challenges.
3. **A store outage is now 503, not a refusal** (S3a). When Redis cannot answer, the token endpoint, the
   challenge endpoint and the attester answer 503 `temporarily_unavailable` (RFC 6749 §5.2) where 0.3.0
   answered 401 "replay" or `use_attestation_challenge`. Anything that alerts on a rise in 401s from attested
   clients should alert on 503s too; a client should retry the same request later rather than fetch a new
   challenge. The automatic-registration filter still answers 500 `server_error` for an outage.
4. **Attestations no longer carry the evidence; read the digest** (S3b). `workload.svid` (every SPIFFE-shaped
   evidence type) and `workload.instance_attestation` (the wallet WIA) are gone from minted attestations and
   from everything downstream of them: the PDP request's `attestation.workload` and any token attribute
   mapped from the verified attestation. In their place, `workload.instance_attestation_sha256` (SHA-256 of
   the evidence as presented, lower-case hex), `workload.instance_attestation_type` (the evidence type id)
   and `workload.instance_attestation_exp`. Consumers this repository cannot edit, from what their names
   say they hold (none of them could be opened from here, so check each):
   - **pf-oidf-modules**: the harness text and the explainers that print or describe `workload.svid` must
     print the digest and say what it is; a harness that asserts the SVID is in the attestation now fails,
     and one that re-verifies the SVID from the attestation has nothing to re-verify - compare
     `instance_attestation_sha256` with `sha256sum` of the token it presented instead. Its terraform's
     access-token mappings and PAZ policies that name `workload.svid` get nothing; map or match the three
     new members.
   - **domain-authority**: traces that show `workload.svid` in the minted attestation show the digest now;
     any trace that expected the raw token to reach the PDP is out of date.
   - **the connector**: its copy of `InstanceIdentity` must take the nine-argument shape (evidence type,
     digest, audiences) or keep the six-argument one and accept that its attestations carry
     `instance_attestation_exp` alone and are not bound; either way it must stop putting the token in
     `workloadClaims`.
5. **Evidence binds to its first presenter** (S3b). After the key proof verifies, the evidence's digest is bound
   to the instance key's thumbprint and the client for as long as the evidence lives. Presenting evidence
   already bound to another key or client is 401 `instance_attestation_bound` and an
   `attestation.evidence.conflict` audit event. Two things change for a workload: one that regenerates its
   instance key while still holding the same evidence must fetch fresh evidence first (SPIRE hands out a new
   JWT-SVID per fetch; a projected cloud token is the same file until it rotates), and a deployment that shares
   one piece of evidence between several instances or clients cannot any more. With no Redis the binding is
   per node, so a thief can still win on a node that has not seen the rightful holder: a clustered
   deployment needs the shared store.
6. **Evidence may live at most a day, and the attestation never outlives it** (S3b). Evidence with more than
   `OIDF_ATTESTER_MAX_EVIDENCE_LIFETIME_SECONDS` (86400) of life left is refused with `invalid_svid` /
   `invalid_instance_attestation` naming the variable; production may set the value lower, never higher
   (a higher value is a 500 `server_error` naming the variable from the first request), a rig may with
   `OIDF_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE=development`. The minted attestation's `exp` and the `expires_in` in the response
   are capped at the evidence's `exp`: a client whose `attestation_issued_ttl` exceeds its evidence's
   remaining life gets a shorter attestation than it asked for. `OIDF_ATTESTER_REQUIRE_SINGLE_AUDIENCE_EVIDENCE=true`
   additionally refuses evidence whose `aud` names more than one party; the default is `false`.
7. **`OIDF_ATTESTER_CIMD_URL` does nothing outside development** (M-1). Unless `OIDF_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE=development`,
   the attester leaves the CIMD source out with an ERROR naming the variable, the discovery document's
   `resolver_plugins_active` omits `cimd`, and clients only that document describes cannot be issued
   attestations; clients from the federation and PingFederate sources are unaffected. A deployment that
   relied on CIMD for production clients has no supported path until X-B02 (Phase 5) lands; move those
   clients to PingFederate's client store or to a federation entity.

## Package EVIDENCE: the store, the digest and the binding

Plan items S3a, S3b and M-1. Verified 2026-09-27 on PingFederate 13.1.3's libraries; the stores against
`redis:7-alpine`, plaintext and TLS.

- **The store** (S3a). `MiniRedisClient` sets the HTTPS endpoint identification algorithm and the SNI name on
  every `rediss://` socket, runs `startHandshake()` before the first byte of `AUTH`, and builds its trust
  from `OIDF_REDIS_CA_FILE` when set. Against a Redis with a self-signed certificate for `localhost`: the
  handshake completes with the CA file; a URL by `127.0.0.1` fails endpoint identification before `AUTH`;
  without the CA file the JVM's CAs refuse the certificate. `redis://` is refused under the production
  profile, read directly from `OIDF_DEPLOYMENT_PROFILE` until PR-1 (Phase 2) centralises it. The three
  stores answer in three states; `RedisAttestationStore` is one view per `StoreNamespace` over one shared
  client, and `AttestationSupport` hands out the view each surface asks for. The verifier, the token-endpoint
  filter (`statusFor`), the challenge endpoint and `OpenIdFederationServlet`'s jti guard map
  `STORE_UNAVAILABLE` to 503. The live tests (`RedisLiveTest`) skip without `OIDF_TEST_REDIS_URL`; the
  Redis service in CI is R-CI5.
- **The digest and the binding** (S3b). `InstanceIdentity` carries `evidenceType`, `evidenceDigest` and
  `audiences`; the seven SPIFFE-shaped validators and the wallet validator fill them and no longer put the
  token in `workloadClaims`; `AttestationMinter` writes the three `instance_attestation_*` members and caps
  `exp` at the evidence's. `AttestationIssuanceServlet` applies `EvidencePolicy`, binds through
  `EvidenceBindingStore`, refuses a conflict with `instance_attestation_bound` and emits
  `attestation.evidence.conflict` through `FederationEvents` (installing `PfAuditEventSink` at init so it
  reaches `audit.log`). Its proof jtis and bindings live under `oidf:cas:*`; its challenges are consumed
  from `oidf:as:challenge:*` until S4b gives the CAS a challenge endpoint of its own. Residual risk, as the
  plan states it: a thief who uses the evidence first wins; detectable, not preventable, without key-bound
  evidence.
- **CIMD** (M-1). `AttesterResolvers.fromEnvironment` takes the environment as a function and honours
  `OIDF_ATTESTER_CIMD_URL` only in development. Refusing the one source rather than the attester keeps the
  blast radius to the clients that source describes, and a source silently dropped is what the ERROR line
  and the discovery document are there to make visible.
