# 0.3.0 - OpenID Federation, complete

0.3.0 makes PingFederate a full member of an OpenID Federation 1.0 federation: it checks chains the way the
Final text says, registers relying parties and agents at every endpoint they arrive at, issues and requires
Trust Marks, answers every federation endpoint, can ask a policy engine, logs its decisions the way
PingFederate logs its own, and passes the OpenID Foundation suite's two federation plans that apply to an OP.
[docs/federation](../federation/README.md) explains it all in plain language.

**Read "Before you deploy" first.** Several changes stop things working until something is done.

## Before you deploy

1. **Declare the extended properties.** PingFederate drops an extended property it hasn't been told about,
   without a word, and 0.3.0 relies on ten of them
   ([docs/extended-properties.json](../extended-properties.json)). A registration whose client comes back
   without `status` is refused and the client disabled - without it a federation client looks like one an
   administrator made, never expired and never checked. The conformance rig declares them in
   `conformance/terraform/extended-properties.tf`.
2. **Apply the migrations** to the authority's database, if hosted agents, Trust Mark grants or the key history
   live in one: `V101__hosted_entity_actor.sql` (without it every enrolment and status change fails),
   `V102__trust_mark.sql` and `V103__federation_key_history.sql`.
3. **Decide what happens to clients registered before 0.3.0.** They have no recorded expiry, so they count as
   expired. An automatic one renews on its next request and carries on; an explicit one is refused until its
   relying party registers again. To give relying parties time, set `OIDF_REGISTRATION_EXPIRY_ENFORCEMENT=log`
   for the migration - expiries are recorded and nothing is enforced, the background sweeper included - and set
   it back afterwards.
4. **Re-assemble `pf-runtime.war`** with this release's `assemble-pf-runtime-war.sh`. It maps the new
   front-channel registration filter; a war assembled by an older script registers nobody at the authorization
   or PAR endpoints.

## What changes for clients

- **The token endpoint is fail-closed.** A registration that fails, or an expired one that can't be renewed, is
  answered with a 401 or 503 and the reason, instead of being passed on to PingFederate.
  `OIDF_AUTO_REGISTRATION_FAIL_CLOSED=false` restores the old behaviour.
- **Every registration ends** (§12.3): at its chain's expiry or after a day, whichever is sooner. An explicit
  registration now often lasts an hour; its relying party registers again before the response's `exp`.
- **Scopes and response types are restricted** to what was registered. They weren't before: PingFederate's
  restrict flags were never set, so the lists did nothing.
- **Explicit registration answers 200**, not 201, with the relying party's own keys in the response, as §12.2.3
  says.
- **Relying parties can register at the authorization and PAR endpoints**, with a signed request object or a
  PAR client assertion, and prove themselves again on every request after (§12.1.1). A relying party that
  publishes keys for `openid_relying_party` is registered with those keys, at every endpoint, not with its
  Federation Entity Keys.
- **The OGNL criterion refuses an expired registration** before it looks at the chain, so a deployment that
  gates tokens with the criterion alone enforces §12.3 too.
- **A chain a client presents is checked as it stands.** Nothing is fetched on its say-so; a chain that doesn't
  hold together on its own is found by discovery instead.

## What changes for the federation

- **The entity configuration describes the OP as PingFederate's own discovery does**, with federation's and
  attestation's parameters on top. It used to carry a handful of endpoints and none of the parameters OpenID
  Connect Discovery requires, which failed every module of the suite's deployed-entity plan.
- **Fetch takes `sub` alone**, list takes its filters, **resolve returns a signed `resolve-response+jwt`**, and
  every error is §8.9 JSON with its status.
- **Constraints, `crit`, peer chains and several trust anchors** are handled; metadata policy follows the Final
  text's merge rules exactly.
- **Trust Marks** are verified, can be required per role (`OIDF_FEDERATION_REQUIRED_TRUST_MARKS`) and can be
  issued (`OIDF_FEDERATION_TRUST_MARK_TYPES`, grants through `/federation/admin/trust-marks`).
- **Historical keys** are published when `OIDF_FEDERATION_HISTORICAL_KEYS=true`.
- **Hosted agents have a lifecycle**: `/federation/admin/entities` suspends, reactivates, revokes, re-keys and
  updates them, and records who did.
- **A policy engine can decide** registrations, enrolments and token issuance over AuthZEN 1.0
  (`OIDF_PDP_MODE=authzen`). It can refuse or narrow, never widen; no answer is a 503 unless it fails open.
- **Federation endpoints can require client authentication** (`OIDF_FEDERATION_ENDPOINT_AUTH`), off by default.
- **PingFederate can be its own trust anchor** without pinning its own keys (`OIDF_FEDERATION_SELF_ANCHOR`).

## What changes for operators

- **Decisions are logged as events**: registrations, renewals, expiries and refusals, the policy engine's
  answers, changes to hosted agents and Trust Mark grants, key rotation, and client authentication at the
  federation endpoints. Each is one line in `server.log`, and the security-relevant ones go to PingFederate's
  `audit.log` too. No token reaches an event line: anything shaped like one is replaced with a hash of it.
  [Operations](../federation/operations.md#reading-the-logs) lists them. The "Automatically registered federation
  client" line the demo reads is unchanged.
- **The federation settings are parsed strictly.** A value that doesn't parse stops the component starting, where
  it used to mean the default, quietly. `OIDF_EVENTS_AUDIT` fails safe instead: anything but `true` or `false`
  leaves audit logging on, and says so.
- **The switches that guard something are `true` or `false`.** `OIDF_REQUIRE_METADATA_POLICY`,
  `OIDF_ATTESTATION_REQUIRE_BRIDGE_KEY` and `OIDF_ATTESTATION_REQUIRE_ATTESTER_BINDING` used to read a typo like
  `yes` as `false`, switching the protection off; now it stops PingFederate starting.
- **`OIDF_FEDERATION_ATTESTER_JWKS` must hold public keys.** It is published in the Entity Configuration, and a
  private or symmetric key there used to be published with it. Now it stops PingFederate starting.
- **`OIDF_TRUST_CONTROLLER_HOST`, `OIDF_TRUST_ANCHOR_JWKS` and `OIDF_TRUST_CONTROLLER_IGNORE_SSL` are
  deprecated** for their `OIDF_FEDERATION_` names. The old names still work, with a warning in the log at
  start-up; setting both to different values stops PingFederate starting.
- **`token_endpoint_auth_method` is no longer written as an extended property.** PingFederate 13.1 counts it as
  standard client metadata and refuses to declare it.

[configuration](../federation/configuration.md) lists every setting, and the
[pf-integration README](../../servlets/pf-integration/README.md#upgrading-from-020) has each upgrade note in
full.

## If you run the conformance rig

A PingFederate 13.1 archive authored fresh accepts only its issuer as a client assertion's audience; one
upgraded from 13.0 also accepts the token endpoint, which the suites' ordinary clients send. The rig now sets
`Rfc7523bisCompliantAudienceVerification` off in `conformance/config-store/`, and `export.sh` refuses an archive
without it. Without it the SSF plan passes 1 of 19 on a fresh archive.

## Conformance

On PingFederate 13.1.3, with the suite at release-v5.3.1: the deployed-entity plan 5 of 5 and the OP plan 20
of 20, all WARNING on PingFederate's vendor metadata, none FAILED. FAPI 2.0 and SSF are unchanged. See
[conformance](../federation/conformance.md).
